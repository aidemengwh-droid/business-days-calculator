What does "5 business days" mean?

Five business days equals one full work week (Monday through Friday). Because it is a complete week, the day of the week stays the same — a Monday lands on the next Monday, a Tuesday on the next Tuesday, and so on.

The result only shifts if a public holiday falls within that week. Each holiday pushes the result forward by one calendar day, turning a 7-calendar-day span into 8 or 9 days.

When is "5 business days" used?

  • Standard processing: applications, permits, and administrative reviews often quote a 5-business-day turnaround.
  • Shipping: free or standard shipping tiers frequently promise delivery within 5 business days.
  • Payment terms: Net 5 invoicing means payment is due 5 business days after the invoice date.
  • Notices: many employment and contract notices require a 5-day warning period.

5 business days vs 5 calendar days

Five calendar days is exactly 5 days from today, including the weekend. Five business days always spans at least 7 calendar days (one full week) and can stretch to 9 or 10 days if a weekend and holiday both fall in the window. Always check which one a deadline refers to.

Common Uses for 5-Business-Day Deadlines

  • Shipping and delivery: Many retailers and carriers use 5-business-day shipping windows for standard or expedited delivery.
  • Contractual deadlines: Legal documents often specify response or action periods in business days.
  • Banking transactions: ACH transfers and check clearing may take 5 business days to complete.
  • Government processing: Permit applications, license renewals, and other administrative processes frequently quote 5-business-day processing times.

Tips for Meeting 5-Business-Day Deadlines

To avoid missing a 5-business-day deadline, start the clock as soon as possible, verify whether holidays extend the deadline, and confirm whether the start date is inclusive or exclusive.
