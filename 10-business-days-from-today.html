What does "10 business days" mean?

Ten business days equals two full work weeks. Like the 5-day case, the day of the week stays the same — a Monday lands two Mondays later. The calendar span is normally 14 days, but any public holiday in the window pushes it forward by one day each.

Because two weeks is long enough to cross a holiday in many months (especially around Thanksgiving, Christmas, or New Year), the actual result can easily land 15 or 16 calendar days out.

When is "10 business days" used?

  • Contract terms: many service agreements specify a 10-business-day performance or response window.
  • Legal notices: statutory notice periods for certain filings and objections run in business days.
  • Project milestones: two-week sprints and deliverables are often quoted as 10 business days.
  • Returns & refunds: some retailers give 10 business days to process a return.

Common Uses for 10-Business-Day Deadlines

  • Shipping and delivery: Many retailers and carriers use 10-business-day shipping windows for standard or expedited delivery.
  • Contractual deadlines: Legal documents often specify response or action periods in business days.
  • Banking transactions: ACH transfers and check clearing may take 10 business days to complete.
  • Government processing: Permit applications, license renewals, and other administrative processes frequently quote 10-business-day processing times.

Tips for Meeting 10-Business-Day Deadlines

To avoid missing a 10-business-day deadline, start the clock as soon as possible, verify whether holidays extend the deadline, and confirm whether the start date is inclusive or exclusive. If the deadline is critical, build in a buffer of one or two extra days to account for unexpected delays. Always use a business day calculator rather than counting manually to avoid errors.
