What does "30 business days" mean?

Thirty business days equals roughly six full work weeks. In pure calendar terms that is about 42 days (six weeks), but because a six-week span almost always crosses at least one public holiday, the real result is usually 43–45 calendar days from today — around a month and a half.

30 business days is the longest common business-day deadline. It is distinct from "30 calendar days" (exactly one month), which does not skip weekends or holidays.

When is "30 business days" used?

  • Net 30 invoicing: the most common payment term — though note "Net 30" usually means 30 calendar days. Always confirm which applies.
  • Probation periods: some employment and trial periods run 30 business days.
  • Statutory deadlines: certain regulatory filings and appeal windows use 30-business-day limits.
  • Project phases: six-week development or review cycles map to 30 business days.

30 business days vs 30 calendar days

This is a frequent source of confusion. 30 calendar days is exactly one month from today, including every weekend and holiday. 30 business days skips all weekends and holidays, landing roughly 42–45 calendar days out. If a contract says "Net 30" without specifying, it almost always means calendar days — but check the fine print, because some industries default to business days.

Understanding 30 Business Days

30 business days equals exactly 6 full work weeks. This is a common timeframe used in shipping, contracts, and administrative processes. When someone says "30 business days," they mean 30 working days — Monday through Friday — excluding public holidays. The exact calendar date depends on which day you start counting and whether any holidays fall within the period.

Common Uses for 30-Business-Day Deadlines

  • Shipping and delivery: Many retailers and carriers use 30-business-day shipping windows for standard or expedited delivery.
  • Contractual deadlines: Legal documents often specify response or action periods in business days.
  • Banking transactions: ACH transfers and check clearing may take 30 business days to complete.
  • Government processing: Permit applications, license renewals, and other administrative processes frequently quote 30-business-day processing times.

Tips for Meeting 30-Business-Day Deadlines

To avoid missing a 30-business-day deadline, start the clock as soon as possible, verify whether holidays extend the deadline, and confirm whether the start date is inclusive or exclusive. If the deadline is critical, build in a buffer of one or two extra days to account for unexpected delays. Always use a business day calculator rather than counting manually to avoid errors.
